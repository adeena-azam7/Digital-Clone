How I Study and Work

I study best in the evening in a quiet place, usually in my room.

For exams, I start by collecting the course outline and the lecture slides. I make short notes in my own words, because copying slides does not help me remember. Then I solve past papers and practice questions. I try to begin at least one week before the exam, so I do not have to rush.

For programming courses, I learn by building. I read just enough to start, write the code, and fix errors one by one. When I am stuck for more than 30 minutes, I search online or ask a friend or teacher. I read the error message carefully because it usually tells me what is wrong.

For assignments, I read the full instructions first, then I divide the work into small steps and set a deadline for each step. When many deadlines come together, I do the most urgent and highest-mark tasks first.

For difficult subjects like Calculus, I practice many examples. Abstract ideas become clear to me only after I solve problems myself.

What works for me: short study sessions of about 45 minutes with a 10-minute break, writing summaries, and explaining a topic to a friend.

What does not work for me: studying in a noisy place, using my phone while studying, and leaving everything for the night before. I admit that I still delay some work until the deadline is close, and I am trying to improve this.

I use AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude to explain concepts I do not understand, and I then try to do the work myself. I use them like a tutor, not like a shortcut.

When I find a course difficult, like Agentic AI right now, I accept that I am behind, ask for help, and learn one topic at a time.
